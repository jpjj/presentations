## Why I left a stable job

<br>

<v-clicks>

- 6 years at the same company &mdash; I wanted **broader experience**.
- I had a resignation period of 6 months. I quit, then started searching for jobs.
- After 3 months and countless interviews I decided to try out freelancing.
- I wanted to **apply OR**, and felt being independent would help me get there.

</v-clicks>
 



---

## How year one actually unfolded

<br>

<v-clicks depth="2">

- After announcing my resignation, **a lot of people reached out** asking what was next.
- A former colleague had also just left. Their new company needed help.
  - **First contract secured** (ML, not OR yet).
- In parallel: 
  - revamped my LinkedIn
  - built [jpjsolutions.com](https://jpjsolutions.com)
  - sorted insurances
  - found a tax advisor
- **Word of mouth spread.** More projects came in.

</v-clicks>

<br>

<v-clicks>

# A normal day 
What an OR freelancer actually does


---

## What does the day look like?

<br>

<v-clicks depth="2">

- Depending on utilization, you bill your hours to the client (usually **remote**).
- Two flavors of work:
  - A very **specific OR project** you own end-to-end.
  - The **OR expert inside a bigger team**. This means: dailies, weeklies, alignment with other devs.
- Sometimes your own laptop & stack. Sometimes the client's.

</v-clicks>


---

## Hourly vs. fixed price

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-8 mt-8">

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 rounded-lg" style="background: rgba(0,0,0,0.04)">

### Fixed price
Good when the **scope is very clear**.

- Predictable for both sides
- You carry the estimation risk
- Rewards you for being efficient

</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 rounded-lg" style="background: rgba(0,0,0,0.04)">

### Hourly rate
Good when **requirements need iteration**.

- Honest about discovery cost
- Aligns with agile feedback loops
- Easier to start and adjust

</div>
</v-click>

</div>


---

## And when no client is active?

<br>

<v-clicks>

- The most important task becomes finding **the next** one.
- Networking, content, conversations, follow-ups.
- "Sales" is part of the job &mdash; whether you like it or not.

</v-clicks>

# What makes a good one
lessons after one year in


---

## 1. Listen to your client

<br>

<v-clicks>

- Most clients **don't** say:
  > *"We need someone who solves our CVRP."*
- They say:
  > *"Our last-mile delivery is messy. Couriers seem to be all over the place."*
- Try to step into their shoes. **What do they actually need?**
- When would the client consider the project successful?
- What are risks, uncertainties, show stoppers to the project?

</v-clicks>


---

## 2. Focus on the solution, not the math

I love math. I really, *really* do.

<v-clicks>

But on real projects, **math is rarely the bottleneck.**

The bottleneck is **getting the solution applied in real life.**

</v-clicks>


---
zoom: 0.9
---

## What "applied" actually requires

<br>

<v-clicks depth="2">

- Does the model **sufficiently reflect reality**?
  $\rightarrow$  Requirements Engineering
- Are the stakeholders / end users ready to **accept a machine-made solution**?
  $\rightarrow$  Change Management
- How does the **input data** look? (garbage in, garbage out)
  $\rightarrow$  Data Validation
- What is **realistically possible** from the IT/OR side?
  $\rightarrow$  Expectation Management
- Nobody knows what they want until they see it.
  $\rightarrow$  Iterative Feedback Loops (Agile)
- Somebody has to build the software around the solver.
  $\rightarrow$  Software Engineering
- This software should not contain any bugs.
  $\rightarrow$ (Automated) Testing

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

…and somewhere in there, there's some real OR work.

</v-click>


---

## 3. Know your tools

<v-clicks>

For each task, the right tool:

- Mathematical Optimization
- (Meta-)Heuristics
- Constraint Programming
- Exact Algorithms
- Machine Learning
- ...

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

The client may not even have a problem that OR solves at all.

</v-click>


---

## The freelancer's paradox

<div class="mt-12 text-center text-2xl">

<v-click>

OR is a **small part** of an OR freelancer's actual work.

</v-click>

<br>
<br>

<v-click>

But it's our **USP**.

</v-click>
