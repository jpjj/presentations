# Chapter 1
Why freelancing & how year one looked


---

## Why I left a stable job

<v-clicks>

- 6 years at the same company &mdash; I wanted **broader experience**.
- I wanted to **apply OR**, and felt being independent would help me get there.
- A few interview processes had not worked out beforehand.

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

> The decision was less about leaving DHL, and more about wanting to **shape my own path**.

</v-click>


---

## How year one actually unfolded

<v-clicks depth="2">

- After announcing my resignation, **a lot of people reached out** asking what was next.
- A former colleague had also just left &mdash; their new company needed help. **First contract secured** (ML, not OR yet).
- In parallel: revamped my LinkedIn, built [jpjsolutions.com](https://jpjsolutions.com), sorted insurances, found a tax advisor.
- **Word of mouth spread.** More projects came in &mdash; both short and long term.
- 2025 positioning: *"AI Freelancer"* &mdash; ML, GenAI, OR.
- Start of 2026: **doubled down on OR.**

</v-clicks>


---

## Why I doubled down on OR

<v-clicks>

- It's where I have the **deepest knowledge**.
- OR is a **hidden champion** &mdash; underrated, and more companies could benefit from it.
- For OR projects, clients prefer the **"OR person"** over the *"I can do everything"* generalist.

</v-clicks>


---

## Year one by the numbers

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-12 mt-12">
<div>

<v-clicks>

- **5** paying clients
- **3** OR-related projects
- **1** ML, **1** GenAI
- Engagements from **2 weeks** to **9 months**

</v-clicks>

</div>
<div>

<v-click>

> Year one ≠ year two.
> Start unsharp, then sharpen.

</v-click>

</div>
</div>

# Chapter 2
A normal day as an OR freelancer


---

## What does the day look like?

<v-clicks depth="2">

- Depending on utilization, you bill your hours to the client (usually **remote**).
- Two flavors of work:
  - A very **specific OR project** you own end-to-end.
  - The **OR expert inside a bigger team** &mdash; dailies, weeklies, alignment with other devs.
- Sometimes your own laptop & stack. Sometimes the client's.

</v-clicks>


---

## Hourly vs. fixed price

<div class="grid grid-cols-2 gap-8 mt-8">

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 rounded-lg" style="background: rgba(0,0,0,0.04)">

### Fixed price
Good when the **scope is very clear**.

- Predictable for both sides
- You carry the estimation risk
- Rewards you for being efficient

</div>
</v-click>

<v-click>
<div class="p-6 rounded-lg" style="background: rgba(0,0,0,0.04)">

### Hourly rate
Good when **requirements need iteration**.

- Honest about discovery cost
- Aligns with agile feedback loops
- Easier to start and adjust

</div>
</v-click>

</div>


---

## And when no client is active?

<v-clicks>

- The most important task becomes finding **the next** one.
- Networking, content, conversations, follow-ups.
- "Sales" is part of the job &mdash; whether you like it or not.

</v-clicks>

# Chapter 3
The hardest task: finding clients


---

## OR freelance projects are rare

<v-clicks>

- On freelancermap.de, a **clearly labeled OR project shows up roughly every two months**.
- They *do* exist &mdash; but most companies don't know that what they need is OR.
- When I show people what OR actually means, the response is often:
  > "Oh &mdash; we have a problem like that."

</v-clicks>


---

## Channels that have worked for me

<v-clicks>

- **freelancermap.de** &mdash; rare hits, but low maintenance.
- **LinkedIn** &mdash; long-term presence pays off. Love/hate relationship.
- **Personal & professional network** &mdash; by far the most consistent source.

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

> Three channels. None of them are quick wins. All of them compound.

</v-click>


---

## What clients see when they look at me

<v-clicks>

- **6 years at DHL** &mdash; concrete industry experience.
- **Testimonials from happy clients in management roles** &mdash; trust transfers.

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

These two outweigh "knows Pyomo / Gurobi / OR-Tools" by a wide margin.

</v-click>


---

## A small but high-leverage habit

<v-click>

After every successful project:

> **Ask the happy client for a LinkedIn recommendation.**

</v-click>

<v-clicks>

- Public, attached to your profile, hard to fake.
- Compounds &mdash; each one lowers the trust barrier for the next prospect.
- Easiest to ask **right when the project ends well** &mdash; not six months later.

</v-clicks>

# Chapter 4
What makes a good OR freelancer (my opinion)


---

## 1. Listen to your client

<v-clicks>

- Most clients **don't** say:
  > *"We need someone who solves our CVRP."*
- They say:
  > *"Our last-mile delivery is messy. Couriers seem to be all over the place."*
- Try to step into their shoes. **What do they actually need?**
- Ask questions. Lots of them.

</v-clicks>


---

## 2. Focus on the solution, not the math

I love math. I really, *really* do.

<v-clicks>

But on real projects, **math is rarely the bottleneck.**

The bottleneck is **getting the solution applied in real life.**

</v-clicks>


---
zoom: 0.9
---

## What "applied" actually requires

<v-clicks depth="2">

- Does the model **sufficiently reflect reality**?
  &nbsp;→ Requirements Engineering
- Are the stakeholders / end users ready to **accept a machine-made solution**?
  &nbsp;→ Change Management
- How does the **input data** look? (garbage in, garbage out)
  &nbsp;→ Data Validation
- What is **realistically possible** from the IT/OR side?
  &nbsp;→ Expectation Management
- Nobody knows what they want until they see it.
  &nbsp;→ Iterative Feedback Loops (Agile)

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

…and somewhere in there, there's some real OR work.

</v-click>


---

## Where my time actually goes

<v-clicks>

- Requirements Engineering
- Expectation Management
- Change Management
- Testing
- Data Acquisition
- Solution Validation
- Feedback Iterations
- **Modelling / coding of algorithms** ← the OR bit

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

> Only a **minority** of my work is "doing OR" &mdash; but it's the part that makes me valuable.

</v-click>


---

## 3. Be a problem solver, not a MIP genius

<v-clicks>

For each task, the right tool:

- Mathematical Optimization
- (Meta-)Heuristics
- Constraint Programming
- Exact Algorithms

</v-clicks>

<v-click>

<br>

The client may not even have a problem that OR solves at all.

</v-click>

<v-click>

> Which solver got the job done is **not the client's concern.**
> Trust is.

</v-click>


---

## The freelancer's paradox

<div class="mt-12 text-center text-2xl">

<v-click>

OR is a **small part** of an OR freelancer's actual work.

</v-click>

<br>
<br>

<v-click>

But it's our **USP**.

</v-click>

</div>


---

## Takeaways

<v-clicks depth="2">

1. **Position yourself sharply.** Generalists lose to specialists when the project is specialized.
2. **Trust > tech stack.** Listen first, pitch later.
3. **References compound.** Ask for them while the work is fresh.
4. **Most of the work isn't OR** &mdash; but the OR is what nobody else can do.
5. **Year one is unsharp.** That's fine. Sharpen as you go.

</v-clicks>
